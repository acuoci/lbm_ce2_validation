# Numerical Experiment II — Three-Dimensional Shear-Mode Population Response

## 1. Context and purpose

The central three-dimensional theoretical result is the linear CE2 population-space relation

\[
\boxed{
\delta f_{K,Q}^{(2)}
=
C_0\,\widehat L_Q\,\mathbf q,
}
\]

where

\[
C_0
=
2\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac12\right)c_s^4,
\]

\(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{15}\) is the incompressible velocity-curvature coordinate vector, and

\[
\widehat L_Q:
\mathcal J_{\rm inc}\rightarrow\mathcal K_Q
\]

is the normalized lattice-specific CE2 observability operator.

The theoretical analysis gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}\widehat L_{19}=6,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\widehat L_{27}=7,
\]

and proves that both D3Q19 and D3Q27 have non-zero CE2 kinetic response for every non-trivial transverse single Fourier mode.

The purpose of Experiment II is to test the stronger, population-level statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the kinetic component measured from a full 3-D LBM shear eigenmode converge to }
C_0\widehat L_Q\mathbf q_F
\text{ as }k\rightarrow0?
}
\]

Unlike a comparison of scalar norms, this experiment compares the complete kinetic population vectors. It therefore directly tests the central \(Q\times15\) population operator used throughout the theory.

The experiment is intentionally minimal:

- periodic domain;
- unforced isothermal BGK;
- one transverse Fourier shear mode;
- very small perturbation amplitude;
- D3Q19 and D3Q27 only;
- no turbulence, walls, forcing, or nonlinear flow structures.

---

## 2. Lattice Boltzmann model

For \(Q=19\) or \(27\), evolve

\[
f_i(\mathbf x+\mathbf c_i,t+1)
=
f_i(\mathbf x,t)
-
\frac1\tau
\left[
f_i(\mathbf x,t)-f_i^{eq}(\mathbf x,t)
\right].
\]

Use lattice units,

\[
\Delta x=\Delta t=1,
\qquad
c_s^2=\frac13.
\]

The standard second-order isothermal equilibrium is

\[
f_i^{eq}
=
w_i\rho
\left[
1+
\frac{\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf u}{c_s^2}
+
\frac{(\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf u)^2}{2c_s^4}
-
\frac{|\mathbf u|^2}{2c_s^2}
\right].
\]

The simulation should use exactly the same velocity ordering and weights as the symbolic construction of \(\widehat L_{19}\) and \(\widehat L_{27}\).

This is essential: the theoretical population vectors and numerical population vectors must refer to identical population coordinates.

Use the tensor-product set

(c_x,c_y,c_z)\in\{-1,0,1\}^3.

The weights are the tensor products of the D1Q3 weights

\[
w(0)=\frac23,
\qquad

Equivalently,

\]

w_{\rm axis}=\frac2{27},
\]

\[

\[
w_{\rm corner}=\frac1{216}.
\]

## 4. Linear amplification matrix

For a Fourier perturbation

\[
\delta f_i(\mathbf x,t)
=
\widehat f_i(t)
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x},
\]

the linearized dynamics are

\[
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t+1)
=
\mathsf A_Q(\mathbf k;\tau)
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t),
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf A_Q(\mathbf k;\tau)
=
\mathsf S_Q(\mathbf k)
\mathsf C_Q(\tau).
}
\]

The streaming matrix is

\[
[\mathsf S_Q]_{ii}
=
e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_i},
\]

and the linearized BGK collision matrix is

\[
\mathsf C_Q(\tau)
=
\left(1-\frac1\tau\right)I_Q
+
\frac1\tau P_{eq,Q},
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
(P_{eq,Q})_{ij}
=
w_i
\left[
1+
\frac{\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf c_j}{c_s^2}
\right].
}
\]

For each lattice, direction, polarization and wavenumber, the exact discrete transverse shear eigenmode is obtained from this matrix.

## 5. Wavevector directions

Only three cubic-symmetry-distinct directions are required:

\[
\boxed{[100],\qquad[110],\qquad[111].}
\]

For a periodic cube of side \(N\),

\[
\mathbf k
=
\frac{2\pi}{N}
(m_x,m_y,m_z).
\]

The three direction families are therefore generated by

\[
[100]:
\qquad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,0,0),
\]

\[
[110]:
\qquad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,m,0),
\]

\[
[111]:
\qquad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,m,m).
\]

The physical wavenumber is

\[
k=|\mathbf k|.
\]

The principal study should use several harmonics \(m\) to establish convergence as

\[
k\rightarrow0.
\]

---

## 6. Transverse polarizations

For every direction choose orthonormal vectors satisfying

\[
\mathbf e_\lambda\cdot\widehat{\mathbf k}=0.
\]

Recommended choices are:

### \([100]\)

\[
\widehat{\mathbf k}=(1,0,0),
\]

\[
\mathbf e_1=(0,1,0),
\qquad
\mathbf e_2=(0,0,1).
\]

By cubic symmetry these should be equivalent.

### \([110]\)

\[
\widehat{\mathbf k}
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,0),
\]

with

\[
\mathbf e_1
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1,0),
\]

and

\[
\mathbf e_2=(0,0,1).
\]

These two polarizations need not be assumed equivalent a priori and should both be tested.

### \([111]\)

\[
\widehat{\mathbf k}
=
\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,1,1).
\]

A convenient transverse basis is

\[
\mathbf e_1
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1,0),
\]

\[
\mathbf e_2
=
\frac1{\sqrt6}(1,1,-2).
\]

The numerical results can determine whether the two responses coincide under the lattice symmetry.

The complete minimal set is therefore six direction/polarization combinations, although symmetry-equivalent results may later be omitted from the final paper.

## 7. Exact shear-branch selection

For each \((\mathbf k,\tau)\), solve the \(Q\times Q\) eigenvalue problem

\[
\mathsf A_Qv_j
=
\lambda_jv_j.
\]

The desired eigenmode should be identified by hydrodynamic content.

For a prescribed target polarization \(\mathbf e_\lambda\), compute

\[
\delta\rho_j
=
\sum_i v_{j,i},
\]

and

\[
\delta\mathbf j_j
=
\sum_i\mathbf c_i v_{j,i}.
\]

Define a polarization overlap such as

\[
S_j
=
\frac{
|\mathbf e_\lambda\cdot\delta\mathbf j_j|
}{
\|\delta\mathbf j_j\|+\epsilon_s
}.
\]

The selected mode should have:

- large \(S_j\);
- negligible density perturbation;
- negligible longitudinal momentum;
- eigenvalue belonging to the hydrodynamic shear branch.

The selection should use the target polarization explicitly because two shear branches are present in three dimensions.

Normalize the selected eigenvector so that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf e_\lambda\cdot\delta\mathbf j=1.
}
\]

The arbitrary complex phase does not affect any weighted error norm.

---

## 8. Exact eigenmode initialization

Let

\[
v_s(\mathbf k,\tau,\lambda)
\]

be the selected complex shear eigenvector.

Initialize the real populations by

\[
\boxed{
f_i(\mathbf x,0)
=
w_i\rho_0
+
\varepsilon
\operatorname{Re}
\left[
v_{s,i}
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}
\right].
}
\]

Recommended values are

\[
\rho_0=1,
\]

and

\[
\varepsilon=10^{-6}.
\]

The exact eigenmode initialization eliminates kinetic startup transients and ensures that, in the linear limit,

\[
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t)
=
\lambda_s^t
\widehat{\mathbf f}(0).
\]

A short run of approximately

\[
5\text{--}10
\]

collision-streaming steps is sufficient.

---

## 9. Fourier extraction

At every time step extract the complex population coefficient at the initialized wavevector:

\[
\widehat f_i(\mathbf k,t)
=
\frac1{N^3}
\sum_{\mathbf x}
\left[
f_i(\mathbf x,t)-w_i\rho_0
\right]
e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.
\]

The use of the complex Fourier coefficient is essential because the discrete eigenvector generally contains population-dependent phases.

From \(\widehat{\mathbf f}\), obtain the measured density and momentum amplitudes,

\[
\widehat\rho
=
\sum_i\widehat f_i,
\]

\[
\widehat{\mathbf j}
=
\sum_i\mathbf c_i\widehat f_i.
\]

At the small perturbation amplitudes used here,

\[
\widehat{\mathbf U}
=
\frac{\widehat{\mathbf j}}{\rho_0}.
\]

---

## 10. Population-space projectors

Use the same weighted equilibrium-Hessian metric as in the theory:

\[
\|g\|_{w,Q}^2
=
g^\dagger W_Q^{-1}g.
\]

Construct

\[
P_{\le2,Q}
\]

from the discrete Hermite population basis through order two and define

\[
\boxed{
P_{K,Q}
=
I_Q-P_{\le2,Q}.
}
\]

The linear Fourier nonequilibrium population is

\[
\widehat{\mathbf f}^{neq}
=
(I-P_{eq,Q})\widehat{\mathbf f}.
\]

The measured kinetic response is then

\[
\boxed{
g_Q^{LBM}
=
P_{K,Q}
\widehat{\mathbf f}^{neq}.
}
\]

Since \(P_{K,Q}P_{eq,Q}=0\), one may equivalently evaluate

\[
g_Q^{LBM}
=
P_{K,Q}\widehat{\mathbf f},
\]

but the explicit nonequilibrium form should be retained in the implementation for clarity.

---

## 11. Fourier curvature vector

For a Fourier velocity amplitude

\[
\mathbf U e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x},
\]

the second derivatives are

\[
\partial_\beta\partial_\gamma u_\alpha
=
-k_\beta k_\gamma U_\alpha
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.
\]

Thus the Fourier curvature tensor is

\[
\boxed{
J_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{F}
=
-k_\beta k_\gamma U_\alpha.
}
\]

Using the curvature-coordinate ordering of the theoretical manuscript,

\[
\mathbf q
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u_{x,xy}\\
u_{x,xz}\\
u_{x,yy}\\
u_{x,zz}\\
u_{x,yz}\\
u_{y,xy}\\
u_{y,yz}\\
u_{y,xx}\\
u_{y,zz}\\
u_{y,xz}\\
u_{z,xz}\\
u_{z,yz}\\
u_{z,xx}\\
u_{z,yy}\\
u_{z,xy}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

construct

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf q_F(\mathbf k,\mathbf U)
=
-
\begin{pmatrix}
k_xk_yU_x\\
k_xk_zU_x\\
k_y^2U_x\\
k_z^2U_x\\
k_yk_zU_x\\
k_xk_yU_y\\
k_yk_zU_y\\
k_x^2U_y\\
k_z^2U_y\\
k_xk_zU_y\\
k_xk_zU_z\\
k_yk_zU_z\\
k_x^2U_z\\
k_y^2U_z\\
k_xk_yU_z
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

The measured Fourier momentum should be used for \(\mathbf U\), rather than the nominal initialization amplitude. This removes arbitrary eigenvector normalization from the comparison.

---

## 12. CE2 population prediction

For the BGK model,

\[
\boxed{
C_0
=
2\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac12\right)c_s^4.
}
\]

With

\[
c_s^2=\frac13,
\]

this becomes

\[
C_0
=
\frac{2}{9}
\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac12\right).
\]

The theoretical CE2 kinetic population vector is

\[
\boxed{
g_Q^{CE2}
=
C_0
\widehat L_Q
\mathbf q_F(\mathbf k,\mathbf U).
}
\]

The exact machine-readable \(\widehat L_{19}\) and \(\widehat L_{27}\) matrices generated in the theoretical analysis should be loaded directly by the numerical code.

They should not be retyped manually.

---

## 13. Important Fourier-amplitude convention

For the real initialization

\[
f_i
=
w_i\rho_0
+
\varepsilon
\operatorname{Re}
\left[
v_{s,i}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}
\right],
\]

the extracted \(+\mathbf k\) Fourier coefficient contains a factor \(1/2\):

\[
\widehat f_i(+\mathbf k)
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}v_{s,i}.
\]

No special correction is required if the same measured Fourier momentum \(\widehat{\mathbf U}\) is used to construct \(\mathbf q_F\), because the amplitude factor appears consistently in both \(g_Q^{LBM}\) and \(g_Q^{CE2}\).

This should nevertheless be checked explicitly in the implementation.

---

## 14. Primary population-vector error

The central diagnostic is

\[
\boxed{
E_Q(k)
=
\frac{
\left\|
g_Q^{LBM}
-
g_Q^{CE2}
\right\|_{w,Q}
}{
\left\|
g_Q^{CE2}
\right\|_{w,Q}
}.
}
\]

The theoretical expectation is

\[
\boxed{
E_Q(k)\rightarrow0
\qquad
k\rightarrow0.
}
\]

This is the principal result of Experiment II.

The convergence order should be measured rather than assumed.

Because the theoretical population is a CE2 approximation to the exact discrete shear eigenvector, the first neglected contribution determines the observed power of \(k\). The numerical data should therefore be fitted as

\[
E_Q(k)\sim Ck^p
\]

over the asymptotic range.

---

## 15. Amplitude and directional diagnostics

The vector error should be supplemented by two secondary diagnostics.

### 15.1 Norm ratio

Define

\[
\boxed{
R_Q(k)
=
\frac{
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}
}{
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}
}.
}
\]

The CE2 prediction requires

\[
\boxed{
R_Q(k)\rightarrow1
\qquad
k\rightarrow0.
}
\]

This distinguishes an amplitude error from a directional error.

### 15.2 Population-space alignment

Define

\[
\boxed{
\chi_Q(k)
=
\frac{
\left|
(g_Q^{CE2})^\dagger
W_Q^{-1}
g_Q^{LBM}
\right|
}{
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}
\,
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}
}.
}
\]

Then

\[
0\le\chi_Q\le1,
\]

and the theoretical expectation is

\[
\boxed{
\chi_Q(k)\rightarrow1
\qquad
k\rightarrow0.
}
\]

The absolute value removes the irrelevant global complex phase.

The combination of

\[
E_Q,\qquad R_Q,\qquad\chi_Q
\]

provides a complete but compact characterization:

- \(E_Q\): full relative population-vector error;
- \(R_Q\): amplitude agreement;
- \(\chi_Q\): direction/alignment agreement.

---

## 16. Fourier-injectivity diagnostic

For every tested transverse mode verify

\[
\boxed{
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}>0
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}>0.
}
\]

This is not a numerical proof of Fourier injectivity—the theorem already provides that proof—but it demonstrates that the predicted non-zero kinetic response is present in the actual LBM populations for the representative symmetry directions.

No random sampling of the sphere is required.

---

## 17. D3Q19 versus D3Q27 interpretation

The experiment should **not** be interpreted as expecting D3Q19 to fail for any of the single Fourier modes.

The theory proves

\[
\ker\widehat L_{19}
\cap
\mathcal J_F
=
\{0\},
\]

and similarly for D3Q27.

Thus both lattices must produce a non-zero kinetic response.

Their population vectors differ because

\[
\widehat L_{19}\neq\widehat L_{27}.
\]

In particular:

- D3Q19 retains six planar CE2 channels through lattice-specific mixing;
- D3Q27 retains six planar channels plus the fully mixed \(xyz\) channel.

For directions such as \([111]\), the D3Q27 mixed channel is generally active and provides a particularly useful comparison with D3Q19.

The objective is therefore to validate each lattice against **its own** theoretical operator, not to declare one lattice numerically correct and the other incorrect.

## 18. Recommended parameter set

The experiment should remain compact.

### Domain

A practical starting point is

\[
\boxed{N=48}
\]

or

\[
N=64.
\]

Since the smallest wavenumber scales as

\[
k_{\min}\sim\frac{2\pi}{N},
\]

\(N=64\) is preferable if memory and runtime remain modest.

The experiment is a single-mode test, so no high spatial resolution is otherwise required.

### Relaxation time

The central 3-D operator structure is independent of \(\tau\) after factoring out \(C_0\).

Therefore a full \(\tau\)-sweep is unnecessary.

Use

\[
\boxed{\tau=1}
\]

for the principal experiment.

A single additional value, e.g.

\[
\tau=0.9,
\]

may be used as a consistency check if desired, but it is not essential for the paper.

### Amplitude

Use

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon=10^{-6}}.
\]

A single amplitude check at

\[
10^{-4}
\]

is sufficient if one wishes to confirm tangent-regime independence.

### Harmonics

Use enough harmonics to obtain approximately

\[
5\text{--}7
\]

small-\(k\) points for each direction.

A useful target is

\[
k\lesssim0.5.
\]

Because the magnitude of \(k\) differs among \([100]\), \([110]\), and \([111]\), the maximum harmonic index should be chosen separately if necessary.

---

## 19. Time integration and averaging

Since the state is initialized as an exact discrete eigenmode, the normalized diagnostics should be essentially time-independent in the linear regime.

Run approximately

\[
N_t=5\text{--}8
\]

steps and evaluate the diagnostics at every step.

For each case report the temporal mean of

\[
E_Q,\quad R_Q,\quad\chi_Q,
\]

and retain their temporal standard deviations as implementation checks.

The measured modal decay ratio should also agree with the exact amplification-matrix eigenvalue.

---

## 20. Expected convergence analysis

For each lattice and direction/polarization, fit

\[
E_Q(k)
=
Ck^p
\]

using the smallest wavenumbers that are clearly above the floating-point error floor.

Report the fitted exponent

\[
p.
\]

Do not impose a theoretical value in advance unless it has been derived separately.

Also fit

\[
R_Q(k)-1
\]

and

\[
1-\chi_Q(k)
\]

if useful for diagnosing the source of the error, but these fits need not appear in the final paper unless they add information beyond \(E_Q\).

## 23. Implementation consistency checks

Before accepting the production data, verify automatically:

1. the velocity ordering matches the stored \(\widehat L_Q\);
2. the standard weights sum to unity;
3. \(P_{K,Q}\) is idempotent:
   \[
   P_{K,Q}^2\approx P_{K,Q};
   \]
4. \(P_{K,Q}\) is orthogonal to the Hermite space through order two in the weighted metric;
5. the selected mode has negligible density perturbation;
6. its momentum is transverse to \(\mathbf k\);
7. its momentum is aligned with the requested polarization;
8. the measured timestep decay agrees with the exact eigenvalue;
9. the measured \(E_Q,R_Q,\chi_Q\) are time-independent in the linear regime;
10. the theoretical vector \(g_Q^{CE2}\) is non-zero;
11. results are unchanged when the perturbation amplitude is reduced;
12. the Fourier sign convention matches
    \[
    S_{ii}=e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_i}.
    \]

These checks should be implemented as numerical assertions or recorded diagnostics.

---

## 24. Important interpretation of possible discrepancies

If

\[
E_Q(k)
\not\rightarrow0,
\]

the first possibilities to investigate are implementation conventions rather than failure of the theory:

1. mismatch in population ordering between the numerical lattice and \(\widehat L_Q\);
2. sign mismatch in the Fourier convention;
3. incorrect factor associated with the real-mode Fourier amplitude;
4. inconsistent curvature-coordinate ordering;
5. incorrect definition of the measured velocity amplitude;
6. use of \(P_K\) or \(W_Q\) inconsistent with the theoretical construction.

Only after these have been excluded should the discrepancy be interpreted physically.

---

## 25. What this experiment establishes

Successful convergence would demonstrate numerically that

\[
\boxed{
P_{K,Q}\widehat f^{neq}
=
C_0
\widehat L_Q
\mathbf q_F
+
o(k^2)
}
\]

for actual D3Q19 and D3Q27 BGK shear eigenmodes.

More specifically, it would show that:

1. the CE2 curvature signal is present in actual evolved populations;
2. the full **population direction**, not only its norm, is predicted by \(\widehat L_Q\);
3. D3Q19 and D3Q27 exhibit their theoretically predicted lattice-specific responses;
4. every tested transverse Fourier mode has non-zero kinetic signal;
5. the population-space observability operator has direct numerical meaning beyond its symbolic construction.

This is the principal reason for including Experiment II.

---

## 26. What the experiment deliberately does not test

The experiment does not address:

- arbitrary curvature jets in the nullspace;
- broadband modal cancellation;
- turbulence;
- the HIT \(P/\Omega\) identity;
- nonlinear finite-Mach reconstruction;
- boundaries;
- body forcing;
- MRT, cumulant, or central-moment collisions;
- AMR or resolution sensing.

Those questions are outside the minimal validation required for the present theoretical paper.

---

## 27. Recommended role in the paper

A suitable subsection title is:

> **Three-dimensional validation of the CE2 population observability operator**

The numerical narrative should remain concise:

1. describe the exact shear-mode initialization;
2. state that the complete population vector is compared with
   \[
   C_0\widehat L_Q\mathbf q_F;
   \]
3. show convergence of \(E_Q\) to zero;
4. report \(R_Q\rightarrow1\) and \(\chi_Q\rightarrow1\);
5. emphasize that both D3Q19 and D3Q27 are correctly described by their respective lattice operators.

Combined with Experiment I, this gives a compact numerical validation hierarchy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{D2Q9 finite-}k\text{ spectral theory}
\quad+\quad
\text{D3Q19/D3Q27 population-space CE2 theory}.
}
\]

No more complicated flow is required for the minimal numerical section.

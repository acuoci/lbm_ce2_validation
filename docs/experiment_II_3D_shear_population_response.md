# Numerical Experiment II — Three-Dimensional Shear-Mode Population Response

## 1. Objective

Experiment II tests the central three-dimensional CE2 population-space prediction

$$
\delta f_{K,Q}^{(2)} =
C_0 \widehat L_Q \mathbf q,
$$

with

$$
C_0 =
2\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\right)c_s^4.
$$

Here, $\mathbf q \in \mathbb R^{15}$ is the incompressible velocity-curvature coordinate vector and $\widehat L_Q$ is the lattice-specific CE2 observability operator.

The theoretical analysis gives

$$
\mathrm{rank}\widehat L_{19}=6,
\qquad
\mathrm{rank}\widehat L_{27}=7,
$$

and predicts a non-zero CE2 kinetic response for every non-trivial transverse single Fourier mode on both D3Q19 and D3Q27.

The numerical question is therefore:

**Does the kinetic population vector generated by the full LBM dynamics converge to**

$$
C_0 \widehat L_Q \mathbf q_F
$$

**as $k \rightarrow 0$?**

Unlike a scalar norm comparison, this experiment tests the complete kinetic population vector.

---

## 2. Lattice Boltzmann model

For $Q=19$ or $27$, the BGK scheme is

$$
f_i(\mathbf x+\mathbf c_i,t+1) =
f_i(\mathbf x,t) -
\frac{1}{\tau}
\left[
f_i(\mathbf x,t)-f_i^{eq}(\mathbf x,t)
\right].
$$

Lattice units are used:

$$
\Delta x=\Delta t=1,
\qquad
c_s^2=\frac{1}{3}.
$$

The standard quadratic equilibrium is

$$
f_i^{eq} =
w_i\rho
\left[
1+
\frac{\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf u}{c_s^2} +
\frac{(\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf u)^2}{2c_s^4} -
\frac{|\mathbf u|^2}{2c_s^2}
\right].
$$

The numerical velocity ordering and weights must match exactly those used to construct the machine-readable operators $\widehat L_{19}$ and $\widehat L_{27}$.

---

### D3Q19

The lattice contains:

- one rest velocity;

The weights are

$$
w_0=\frac{1}{3},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{axis}}=\frac{1}{18},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{face}}=\frac{1}{36}.
$$

### D3Q27

The lattice is the tensor-product set

$$
(c_x,c_y,c_z)\in\{-1,0,1\}^3.
$$

The corresponding weights are

$$
w_0=\frac{8}{27},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{axis}}=\frac{2}{27},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{face}}=\frac{1}{54},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{corner}}=\frac{1}{216}.
$$

## 4. Linear amplification matrix

For a Fourier perturbation

$$
\delta f_i(\mathbf x,t) =
\widehat f_i(t)
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x},
$$

the linearized dynamics satisfy

$$
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t+1) =
\mathsf A_Q(\mathbf k;\tau)
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t),
$$

with

$$
\mathsf A_Q(\mathbf k;\tau) =
\mathsf S_Q(\mathbf k)\mathsf C_Q(\tau).
$$

The streaming matrix is diagonal,

$$
[\mathsf S_Q]_{ii} =
e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_i},
$$

and the collision matrix is

$$
\mathsf C_Q(\tau) =
\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)I_Q
+
\frac{1}{\tau}P_{eq,Q},
$$

where

$$
(P_{eq,Q})_{ij} =
w_i
\left[
1+
\frac{\mathbf c_i\cdot\mathbf c_j}{c_s^2}
\right].
$$

For each lattice, direction, polarization, and wavenumber, the exact transverse shear eigenmode is obtained from this matrix.

---

## 5. Wavevector directions and polarizations

The three cubic-symmetry-distinct wavevector families are

$$
[100],
\qquad
[110],
\qquad
[111].
$$

For a periodic cube of side $N$,

$$
\mathbf k =
\frac{2\pi}{N}(m_x,m_y,m_z).
$$

The direction families are generated by

$$
[100]:
\quad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,0,0),
$$

$$
[110]:
\quad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,m,0),
$$

$$
[111]:
\quad
(m_x,m_y,m_z)=(m,m,m).
$$

The scalar wavenumber is

$$
k = |\mathbf k|.
$$

Recommended transverse polarizations are:

| Direction | Polarization 1 | Polarization 2 |
|---|---|---|
| $[100]$ | $(0,1,0)$ | $(0,0,1)$ |
| $[110]$ | $(1,-1,0)/\sqrt{2}$ | $(0,0,1)$ |
| $[111]$ | $(1,-1,0)/\sqrt{2}$ | $(1,1,-2)/\sqrt{6}$ |

The two $[110]$ polarizations are retained separately because they need not be kinetically equivalent.

## 6. Exact shear-mode selection

For each $(\mathbf k,\tau)$, solve

$$
\mathsf A_Q v_j =
\lambda_j v_j.
$$

For a prescribed transverse polarization $\mathbf e_\lambda$, compute

$$
\delta\rho_j =
\sum_i v_{j,i},
$$

and

$$
\delta\mathbf j_j =
\sum_i \mathbf c_i v_{j,i}.
$$

A convenient polarization score is

$$
S_j =
\frac{
|\mathbf e_\lambda\cdot\delta\mathbf j_j|
}{
\|\delta\mathbf j_j\|+\epsilon_s
}.
$$

The selected eigenmode should have:

- large $S_j$;
- negligible density perturbation;
- negligible longitudinal momentum;
- an eigenvalue belonging to the hydrodynamic shear branch.

The eigenvector is normalized so that

$$
\mathbf e_\lambda\cdot\delta\mathbf j = 1.
$$

Its arbitrary complex phase does not affect the weighted population-space diagnostics.

---

## 7. Exact eigenmode initialization

Let $v_s$ denote the selected shear eigenvector. The real-space populations are initialized as

$$
f_i(\mathbf x,0) =
w_i\rho_0
+
\varepsilon
\mathrm{Re}
\left[
v_{s,i}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}
\right].
$$

The production values are

$$
\rho_0=1,
\qquad
\varepsilon=10^{-6}.
$$

This exact eigenmode initialization avoids kinetic startup transients. In the linear regime,

$$
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t) =
\lambda_s^t
\widehat{\mathbf f}(0).
$$

Only a short collision-streaming run is therefore required.

---

## 8. Fourier extraction

At each timestep, extract the complex population coefficient at the initialized wavevector:

$$
\widehat f_i(\mathbf k,t) =
\frac{1}{N^3}
\sum_{\mathbf x}
\left[
f_i(\mathbf x,t)-w_i\rho_0
\right]
e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.
$$

The corresponding density and momentum amplitudes are

$$
\widehat\rho =
\sum_i \widehat f_i,
$$

and

$$
\widehat{\mathbf j} =
\sum_i \mathbf c_i \widehat f_i.
$$

For the small perturbation amplitudes used here,

$$
\widehat{\mathbf U} =
\frac{\widehat{\mathbf j}}{\rho_0}.
$$

The measured Fourier velocity amplitude is used when constructing the theoretical CE2 prediction.

---

## 9. Population-space projectors

The weighted population norm is

$$
\|g\|_{w,Q}^2 =
g^\dagger W_Q^{-1}g.
$$

Let $P_{\le2,Q}$ denote the weighted Hermite projector through second order. The kinetic projector is

$$
P_{K,Q} =
I_Q-P_{\le2,Q}.
$$

The linear Fourier nonequilibrium population is

$$
\widehat{\mathbf f}^{neq} =
(I-P_{eq,Q})\widehat{\mathbf f}.
$$

The measured kinetic population is therefore

$$
g_Q^{LBM} =
P_{K,Q}\widehat{\mathbf f}^{neq}.
$$

Since

$$
P_{K,Q}P_{eq,Q}=0,
$$

the equivalent expression

$$
g_Q^{LBM} =
P_{K,Q}\widehat{\mathbf f}
$$

may also be used.

---

## 10. Fourier curvature vector

For a Fourier velocity field

$$
\mathbf u(\mathbf x) =
\mathbf U e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x},
$$

the second derivatives are

$$
\partial_\beta\partial_\gamma u_\alpha =
-k_\beta k_\gamma U_\alpha
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.
$$

Thus

$$
J_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{F} =
-k_\beta k_\gamma U_\alpha.
$$

Using the curvature-coordinate ordering adopted in the theoretical analysis,

$$
\mathbf q =
\begin{pmatrix}
u_{x,xy} \\
u_{x,xz} \\
u_{x,yy} \\
u_{x,zz} \\
u_{x,yz} \\
u_{y,xy} \\
u_{y,yz} \\
u_{y,xx} \\
u_{y,zz} \\
u_{y,xz} \\
u_{z,xz} \\
u_{z,yz} \\
u_{z,xx} \\
u_{z,yy} \\
u_{z,xy}
\end{pmatrix},
$$

the Fourier curvature vector is

$$
\mathbf q_F(\mathbf k,\mathbf U) = -
\begin{pmatrix}
k_xk_yU_x \\
k_xk_zU_x \\
k_y^2U_x \\
k_z^2U_x \\
k_yk_zU_x \\
k_xk_yU_y \\
k_yk_zU_y \\
k_x^2U_y \\
k_z^2U_y \\
k_xk_zU_y \\
k_xk_zU_z \\
k_yk_zU_z \\
k_x^2U_z \\
k_y^2U_z \\
k_xk_yU_z
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Using the measured $\widehat{\mathbf U}$ removes any dependence on the arbitrary normalization of the eigenvector.

---

## 11. CE2 population prediction

For the BGK model,

$$
C_0 =
2\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\right)c_s^4.
$$

Since $c_s^2=1/3$,

$$
C_0 =
\frac{2}{9}
\rho_0\tau
\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\right).
$$

The theoretical population vector is

$$
g_Q^{CE2} =
C_0
\widehat L_Q
\mathbf q_F(\mathbf k,\mathbf U).
$$

The operators $\widehat L_{19}$ and $\widehat L_{27}$ are loaded directly from the machine-readable CSV files provided with the repository. They should not be retyped manually.

---

## 12. Fourier-amplitude convention

For the real initialization

$$
f_i =
w_i\rho_0
+
\varepsilon
\mathrm{Re}
\left[
v_{s,i}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}
\right],
$$

the extracted positive-wavevector coefficient is

$$
\widehat f_i(+\mathbf k) =
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}v_{s,i}.
$$

No correction is required if the measured Fourier momentum is used consistently to construct $\mathbf q_F$, because the same factor appears in both the numerical and theoretical population vectors.

---

## 13. Population-space diagnostics

The primary diagnostic is the relative population-vector error

$$
E_Q(k) =
\frac{
\left\|
g_Q^{LBM}-g_Q^{CE2}
\right\|_{w,Q}
}{
\left\|
g_Q^{CE2}
\right\|_{w,Q}
}.
$$

The CE2 prediction requires

$$
E_Q(k)\rightarrow0
\qquad
\text{as } k\rightarrow0.
$$

The convergence order is determined from

$$
E_Q(k)\sim Ck^p.
$$

Two additional diagnostics separate amplitude and directional errors.

### Norm ratio

$$
R_Q(k) =
\frac{
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}
}{
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}
}.
$$

The expected limit is

$$
R_Q(k)\rightarrow1.
$$

### Population-space alignment

$$
\chi_Q(k) =
\frac{
\left|
(g_Q^{CE2})^\dagger
W_Q^{-1}
g_Q^{LBM}
\right|
}{
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}
}.
$$

The expected limit is

$$
\chi_Q(k)\rightarrow1.
$$

Thus:

- $E_Q$ measures the full relative population-vector discrepancy;
- $R_Q$ measures amplitude agreement;
- $\chi_Q$ measures population-space alignment.

---

## 14. Fourier-injectivity check

For every tested transverse mode, verify

$$
\|g_Q^{LBM}\|_{w,Q}>0,
$$

and

$$
\|g_Q^{CE2}\|_{w,Q}>0.
$$

This is not a numerical proof of Fourier injectivity. It only confirms that the theoretically predicted non-zero kinetic response is present in the tested LBM modes.

---

## 15. D3Q19 versus D3Q27

The experiment does not test whether one lattice is correct and the other fails.

The theory predicts non-zero response for both lattices:

$$
\ker\widehat L_{19}
\cap
\mathcal J_F =
\{0\},
$$

with the analogous result for D3Q27.

Their population responses differ because

$$
\widehat L_{19}\neq\widehat L_{27}.
$$

D3Q19 retains six planar CE2 channels through lattice-specific mixing, whereas D3Q27 additionally retains the fully mixed $xyz$ channel.

Each lattice must therefore be compared with **its own** CE2 operator.

## 17. Time integration

Because the state is initialized with an exact discrete shear eigenmode, the normalized diagnostics should remain essentially constant in time in the linear regime.

A short run is therefore sufficient. At each timestep, verify that:

$$
\widehat{\mathbf f}(t+1)
\approx
\lambda_s\widehat{\mathbf f}(t),
$$

and that $E_Q$, $R_Q$, and $\chi_Q$ are time-independent within numerical precision.

Temporal averaging may be used to reduce floating-point noise, but no long-time statistical averaging is required.

---

## 18. Convergence analysis

For each lattice and symmetry-distinct mode family, fit

$$
E_Q(k)=Ck^p
$$

over the smallest available wavenumbers.

The exponent $p$ should be measured from the data rather than imposed in advance.

The principal result is the asymptotic convergence

$$
E_Q(k)\rightarrow0,
$$

with the fitted exponent used to characterize the observed convergence class.

Secondary fits of

$$
R_Q(k)-1
$$

and

$$
1-\chi_Q(k)
$$

may be used for diagnosis but are not required for the main result.

## 20. Implementation checks

Before accepting the production results, verify automatically that:

1. the numerical population ordering matches the stored $\widehat L_Q$ operator;
2. the lattice weights sum to unity;
3. the kinetic projector is idempotent;
4. $P_{K,Q}$ is orthogonal to the Hermite space through second order;
5. the selected eigenmode has negligible density perturbation;
6. the mode momentum is transverse to $\mathbf k$;
7. the momentum is aligned with the requested polarization;
8. the measured modal decay agrees with the exact eigenvalue;
9. $E_Q$, $R_Q$, and $\chi_Q$ are time-independent in the linear regime;
10. the CE2 prediction is non-zero;
11. reducing $\varepsilon$ does not change the normalized diagnostics;
12. the Fourier convention is consistent with

$$
[\mathsf S_Q]_{ii} =
e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_i}.
$$

These checks should be implemented as assertions or retained as diagnostic outputs.

---

## 21. Diagnosing discrepancies

If

$$
E_Q(k)\not\rightarrow0,
$$

the first possibilities to investigate are implementation conventions rather than the theoretical model itself:

1. inconsistent population ordering;
2. incorrect Fourier sign convention;
3. incorrect real-mode Fourier factor;
4. inconsistent curvature-coordinate ordering;
5. incorrect measured velocity amplitude;
6. inconsistent definitions of $P_{K,Q}$ or $W_Q$.

Only after these possibilities have been excluded should the discrepancy be interpreted physically.

---

## 22. Interpretation

Successful convergence demonstrates that

$$
P_{K,Q}\widehat f^{neq} =
C_0
\widehat L_Q
\mathbf q_F
+
o(k^2)
$$

for the tested D3Q19 and D3Q27 BGK transverse shear modes.

More specifically, the experiment tests whether:

- the CE2 curvature signal is present in evolved populations;
- the complete population direction is predicted correctly;
- each lattice exhibits its own theoretical population response;
- all tested transverse Fourier modes retain non-zero kinetic signal;
- the symbolic observability operator has direct numerical meaning in the full collision-streaming dynamics.

The experiment is intentionally limited to linear, periodic, unforced shear modes. It does not address turbulence, boundaries, forcing, nonlinear finite-Mach reconstruction, broadband cancellation, MRT/cumulant collision models, or adaptive-resolution effects.

Its role is therefore precise: **to validate the three-dimensional CE2 population observability operator directly at population-vector level.**
